## Relevant page text

The importer is required to present, before import:

- A commercial invoice.
- A bill of lading.
- A certificate of origin, unnecessary when the country of origin is clearly established.

The importer must also present documents required by the nature of the imported goods. Examples on the page include an IECEE certificate for goods subject to that requirement and product registration with the relevant parties where necessary. It directs importers to check product-specific requirements through the Customs Tariff or a customs broker.

The page says importers must complete Fasah clearance procedures, present the necessary documents, and complete the customs declaration at least 48 hours before the shipment arrives at the port of entry. It says data may be submitted directly or through a customs broker once documents are available. Fasah is identified as the platform for tracking the shipment and receiving notifications after declaration creation.

## Scope and caveat

This is ZATCA's current general import guidance, not a universal, exhaustive checklist. The same page explicitly makes further paperwork conditional on the goods and tells users to check tariff/product-specific controls. Do not tell a user that the three listed documents are the only requirements for every commodity or shipment.
